# Financial Research Memorandum: Tencent Holdings Limited (00700.HK)

> Reporting period: six months ended 2025/6/30 (unaudited, reviewed) | Disclosure date: 2025-08-26 | Analysis date: 2026-10-15
> reporting basis: CNY million, consolidated statements, International Financial Reporting Standards (IAS 34), reviewed by PricewaterhouseCoopers with an unqualified conclusion [Current filing PDF p. 23]
> One-sentence judgment: Revenue and gross profit accelerated, and operating cash flow covered consolidated net profit by approximately 1.43 times, but a considerable portion of the profit increment came from investment-related fair value changes and impairment reversals, while capital expenditure and net purchases of investment financial assets rose sharply, so cash resilience needs to be judged together with the investment pace.
> Scope of evidence: the pages attached to the original 2025 interim report for the current period and the 2024 interim report for the prior period; Only the attached physical pages are covered; not obtained market quotes, consensus, and announcement snapshots.

## I. Research Conclusions and Key Changes

**Change one: Revenue and gross profit accelerated in tandem, and the rise in gross margin is the most solid operating signal in the current period.** First-half revenue was 364,526 million, year over year+14% (2024 same period: 320,618 million), gross profit was 205,506 million, year over year+21%, and gross margin rose from 52.9% to 56.4%[Current filing PDF p. 5][Current filing PDF p. 24]. Gross profit grew faster than revenue, indicating a favorable change in revenue mix or unit costs rather than pure scale expansion.

**Change two: Profit attributable to owners grew （+16%）, lower than gross profit growth of （+21%）, with the difference mainly from operating expenses and investment-related gains and losses.** General and administrative expenses were 65,585 million, year over year+25.4% (2024 same period: 52,300 million), including share-based compensation of 18,904 million (2024 same period: 13,016 million) [Current filing PDF p. 19][Current filing PDF p. 24]. At the same time, other gains/(losses), net, turned from +2,515 to -4,975 million[Current filing PDF p. 24], constituting a drag on profit.

**Change three: Non-IFRS profit attributable to owners was 124,381 million, year over year+16%, differing from statutory profit attributable to owners of 103,449 million by 20,932 million, with the difference mainly from non-cash items such as share-based compensation and amortization of intangible assets**[Current filing PDF p. 19]. This metric is a Hong Kong-listed non-GAAP alternative performance measure and is not equivalent to A-share statutory profit excluding non-recurring items net profit.

Judgment condition: If share-based compensation and investment-related gains and losses return to normal in the second half and gross margin is maintained, the improvement in profit quality can continue; if the rise in gross margin mainly comes from a one-off mix shift (such as contraction of low-margin businesses), then its sustainability needs to be verified by next period's segment data.

## II. Operating Drivers and Earnings Quality

**At the segment level, value-added services are the dual main force for revenue and gross profit.** First-half value-added services revenue was 183,501 million, gross profit was 110,134 million, and gross margin was 60.0%; marketing services revenue was 67,615 million, gross profit was 38,279 million, and gross margin was 56.6%; FinTech and Business Services revenue was 110,443 million, gross profit was 56,549 million, and gross margin was 51.2%[Current filing PDF p. 45]. Compared with the same period of 2024: value-added services gross margin was 57.2%, marketing services was 55.2%, and FinTech and Business Services was 46.6%[Current filing PDF p. 45]. Gross margins rose in all three main segments, among which FinTech and Business Services increased by about 4.6 percentage points, making it one of the important sources of the group's gross margin increase.

**However, the segment-weighted contribution bridge was not disclosed in the original text, so no single segment can be designated as the main reason for the overall gross margin increase.** In the chairman's report, management mentioned qualitative drivers such as AI improving advertising effectiveness, commercial payment volume resuming year over year growth, and mini-game gross billings year over year+20% [Current filing PDF p. 6][Current filing PDF p. 7], but did not provide a volume-price breakdown. Revenue growth contribution does not equal gross margin contribution, and management also did not deny a price contribution, so no inference is made that price was zero.

**Profit quality needs to be viewed in layers.** Statutory operating profit was 117,670 million, year over year+14%, in line with revenue growth; non-GAAP operating profit was 138,568 million, year over year+18%[Current filing PDF p. 5]. Among non-GAAP adjustments, share-based compensation was 17,461 million, amortization of intangible assets was 3,129 million, and SSV and CPP were 308 million[Current filing PDF p. 19]. These items are mostly non-cash or investment-related, and excluding them brings profit closer to core operations, but the adjustments themselves also reflect real economic costs (such as equity dilution) and should not simply be regarded as "inflation."

**Alternative explanation:** The gross margin improvement may also come from a revenue mix shift toward higher-margin businesses (such as a higher share of value-added services), rather than from improved unit profitability in each business. If segment gross margins decline in the next period, the sustainability of this period's improvement is questionable.

## III. Asset Quality, Cash, and Debt Servicing

**Operating cash flow covers consolidated net profit well.** First-half net cash flows from operating activities were 151,265 million, consolidated net profit was 105,769 million, and coverage was approximately 1.43 times [Current filing PDF p. 33][Current filing PDF p. 24]. net profit attributable to owners of the parent 103,449 million, and the difference from consolidated net profit was non-controlling interests of 2,320 million[Current filing PDF p. 24]. Operating cash flow was higher than net profit, partly related to the add-back of non-cash expenses such as depreciation and amortization, but the complete profit-to-cash reconciliation statement was not obtained from the attached pages, so depreciation is not identified as a verified main source of the difference.

**Cash outflows from investing activities expanded substantially.** Net cash used in investing activities was 72,407 million (2024 same period: 45,449 million), of which payments for property, equipment and equipment, construction in progress, and investment properties were 45,558 million (2024 same period: 12,552 million), and purchases of financial assets at fair value through profit or loss were 71,170 million[Current filing PDF p. 33]. Capital expenditures (management reporting basis) in the first half was 46,583 million, compared with the 2024 first-half figure of 23,088 million[Current filing PDF p. 15]。 The issuer's definition of capital expenditure may differ from "payments/prepayments for property, equipment and equipment, construction in progress, and investment properties," so they are labeled separately.

**Cash bridge (model calculation, pending manual review):** Beginning cash and cash equivalents 132,519 million + net operating inflow 151,265 million - net investing outflow 72,407 million - net financing outflow 30,111 million + exchange gain 791 million = ending balance 182,057 million[Current filing PDF p. 33][Current filing PDF p. 34]. Calculated using the original statement figures: 132,519+151,265-72,407-30,111+791 = 182,057, consistent with the ending balance presented, with no residual difference.

**Debt and available cash.** As of 2025/6/30, borrowings were 261,597 million, notes payable were 132,218 million, and total debt was 393,815 million; total debt/adjusted EBITDA ratio was 1.29 (2024/12/31: 1.22) [Current filing PDF p. 38]. Cash and cash equivalents were 182,057 million, total time deposits were 261,847 million (non-current 92,424 + current 169,423), and restricted cash was 3,893 million[Current filing PDF p. 26]. Management disclosed net cash/(debt) of 74,592 million[Current filing PDF p. 15]. Restricted cash is not included in available cash; whether time deposits can be freely used needs further confirmation in conjunction with the notes.

**The debt maturity structure for the next 12 months was not fully obtained from the attached pages**, current borrowings were 58,631 million, and current notes payable were 12,880 million[Current filing PDF p. 28], but the specific maturity distribution of the non-current portion needs supplementary verification.

## IV. Risks, Contradictions, and Counterevidence

**Risk one (Observed risk facts): Fluctuations in investment-related gains and losses increase profit uncertainty.** First-half other gains/(losses), net were -4,975 million, of which the "other" item was -6,682 million[Current filing PDF p. 48]; at the same time, investment portfolio returns were 106.47 hundred million, year over year+28%, including fair value gains of 1,909 million and impairment reversals of 1,554 million[Current filing PDF p. 21]. Investment-related gains and losses move in opposite directions and are highly volatile, making statutory profit less predictable than non-GAAP reporting basis. Alternative explanation: These fluctuations may reflect normal portfolio revaluation rather than operational deterioration.

**Risk two (future risk still to be verified): Capital expenditure and purchases of investment financial assets rose sharply, may compress free cash.** First-half payments for property and equipment, etc. were 45,558 million, and purchases of FVTPL financial assets were 71,170 million[Current filing PDF p. 33], while in the same period of 2024 they were 12,552 million and 17,224 million, respectively. If this pace continues, the coverage of investing expenditures by operating cash flow will narrow. Next period's cash flow statement is needed to verify whether this is temporary allocation or a trend increase.

**Risk three (Insufficient evidence): The complete boundary between restricted funds and available cash not obtained.** Restricted cash of 3,893 million has been presented [Current filing PDF p. 26], but whether there are restrictions such as pledges or customer funds within time deposits is not disclosed on the attached pages, so no conclusion can be given that "available cash is sufficient."

## V. Scenarios and Follow-up Tracking

**Sensitive variables: gross margin sustainability and the pace of investment expenditure.** Base scenario (assumption): If segment gross margins in the second half maintain the first-half level and net purchases and sales of investment financial assets return to the 2024 pace, then the coverage of investing expenditures by operating cash flow improves. Downside scenario (assumption): If gross margin falls back to the 2024 level and capital expenditure maintains the first-half pace, then simplified cash balance (operating cash flow minus expenditures on construction of long-term assets) will narrow significantly. The above are analytical assumptions, not company guidance.

**Tracking item one:** 2025 third-quarter results (subsequent actual disclosure that should be supplemented and verified, and should already have been published before the analysis date) — observe whether segment gross margins are maintained and changes in share-based compensation year over year.
**Tracking item two:** 2025 annual report — observe full-year operating cash flow and net investment expenditure, the note on debt maturity structure, and details of restricted funds.
**Tracking item three:** Subsequent interim report — observe whether the direction of investment portfolio fair value changes and impairment reversals reverses.

With no actual market quotes, consensus, or forecast inputs, valuation and expectation gap are omitted.
